OUR STORY · WHY HOMEWARD EXISTS

I went looking for a faith I could actually live.

I grew up evangelical and genuinely loved Jesus. But some of the answers I was given stopped making sense to me, and I wasn’t sure what to do with them.

If God is love, is everyone who doesn’t believe exactly what I believe really going to hell?

I could not find answers that satisfied both my mind and my heart. So I kept asking. Then I went looking.

That search eventually led through years of study, monasteries, retreat centers, extended silent practice, and contemplative communities across traditions—and, unexpectedly, back toward Christ.

THE QUESTIONS BEGAN EARLY

I loved Jesus. I wasn’t sure what to do with the answers I was given.

I grew up evangelical. There was much in that world I still value: Scripture, prayer, service, the person of Jesus, and the conviction that faith should matter.

But some questions would not leave me alone. What happens to people in other religions? Is fear really the center of salvation? Why did a God of love sometimes sound so unlike the Jesus I encountered in the Gospels?

I could not find answers that satisfied both my mind and my heart. So I kept asking. Then I went looking.

I wasn’t trying to lose my faith. I was trying to be honest about the questions I already had—and to find a way of faith I could live truthfully.

SO I WENT LOOKING

I wanted to know what actually changes a human life.

Over the years I spent months in retreat environments, including extended silent practice, monasteries and retreat centers, and communities shaped by Christian meditation, Buddhist mindfulness, contemplative prayer, and other traditions. This wasn’t casual curiosity. I wanted to understand what sustained practice actually does to a life.

You don’t need any of this experience to begin. One reason I’m building Homeward is so people can taste the depth of these practices in ordinary life. No meditation experience or theological expertise is required. Start where you are.

THE RETURN

My heart kept coming back to Christ.

Through all of it, I never really stopped praying to Jesus. My old framework for Christianity changed, but the person and way of Christ remained the deepest gravitational center of my spiritual life.

Then I read Richard Rohr’s The Universal Christ. It was a pivotal moment for me. His expansive, Christ-centered reframing gave me a vision of Christianity I had been missing—one spacious enough for mystery and honest questions while bringing me more deeply back toward Jesus.

The practices I encountered elsewhere did not replace Christ for me. They helped me return with a more open heart.

A TURNING POINT

The Universal Christ

It gave me Christian language for a vision of Christ, creation, salvation, and divine love that felt both deeply rooted and radically open.

“If I had heard Christianity framed this way earlier, I’m not sure I would have felt I needed to leave it to find depth.”

THE MISSING PIECE

I came home with practices. I didn’t have people to practice with.

A retreat can open something in you. Then you come home, the phone rings, work starts, the kids need something, traffic happens—and the practice can quietly disappear.

What I wanted was remarkably simple: a few people who wanted to keep going. A place to practice together, explore Scripture honestly, carry something into the week, come back and talk about what actually happened, and support one another with gentle accountability.

Retreat can deepen a practice. Community helps you live it. Homeward is meant to be that home base—a place to return to, whether you are just beginning or eventually want to go deeper.

That is why I’m starting Homeward.

WHY I FEEL CALLED TO DO THIS

What I received, I want to pass on.

So much of what has shaped my life was freely given to me by teachers, mentors, retreat leaders, writers, spiritual communities, and people who simply made space for me to learn.

Over time I began to feel that keeping all of that for myself would miss something important. The best way I know to honor the people who taught me is to pass forward what I can of what they gave me.

Homeward is one expression of that gratitude.

My hope is to open a doorway into practices that can sometimes feel hidden away in monasteries, retreat centers, or specialized communities—and help people carry them into ordinary life, make them their own, and eventually pass them on to others.

Homeward is…

  • Jesus-centered and open to sincere seekers.
  • Practice-centered, not agreement-centered.
  • Built around honest conversation and lived experience.
  • A place to ask real questions without being pushed toward a prescribed doctrinal conclusion.
  • Currently no cost to participate.

Homeward is not…

  • A replacement for your church, therapy, or professional care.
  • A debate club where someone has to win.
  • A claim that every tradition teaches the same thing.
  • A place where spiritual experience or expertise is required.
  • A finished institution pretending it already has everything figured out.

The retreat centers, traditions, books, and teachers referenced here are part of my personal spiritual history. Their inclusion does not imply endorsement of Homeward.
