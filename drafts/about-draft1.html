OUR STORY · WHY HOMEWARD EXISTS

I went looking for a faith I could actually live.

I grew up loving Jesus, but some of the answers I inherited stopped making sense to me. So I kept asking questions. Those questions eventually led me into years of study, monasteries, retreat centers, extended silent practice, and contemplative communities across traditions.

If God is love, is everyone who doesn’t believe exactly what I believe really going to hell?

What surprised me was where the search eventually led me: back toward Christ.

THE QUESTIONS BEGAN EARLY

I loved Jesus. Some of the answers stopped holding together.

I’m grateful for much of the faith I inherited: Scripture, prayer, service, and especially Jesus. But questions kept surfacing that I couldn’t honestly ignore.

What about people born into other religions? Is fear really at the center of salvation? Why did some descriptions of God seem so different from the life and character of Jesus?

I wasn’t trying to lose my faith. I was trying to find a way to live it truthfully.

That search led me to Religious Studies and Anthropology at UC Santa Barbara—and eventually beyond books into practice itself.

SO I WENT LOOKING

I wanted to know what actually changes a human life.

Over the years I spent months in retreat environments, including extended silent practice, monasteries and retreat centers, and communities shaped by Christian meditation, Buddhist mindfulness, contemplative prayer, and other traditions. This wasn’t casual curiosity. I wanted to understand what sustained practice actually does to a life.

START WHERE YOU ARE

You don’t need any of that experience to begin.

I don’t believe everyone needs to spend ten days in silence or a month at a retreat center. One reason I’m building Homeward is so people can taste the depth of these practices in ordinary life—starting exactly where they are.

No meditation experience required. No theological expertise required. Start where you are.

THE RETURN

The surprising part was that I kept coming back to Christ.

Through all of my searching, I never really stopped praying to Jesus. My old framework for Christianity changed, but Christ remained somewhere near the center.

Then I read Richard Rohr’s The Universal Christ. It was a pivotal moment for me. His expansive, Christ-centered reframing gave me a vision of Christianity I had been missing: spacious enough for mystery and honest questions, while bringing me more deeply back toward Jesus.

It didn’t give me every answer. It gave me a way home.

A TURNING POINT

The Universal Christ

It gave me Christian language for a vision of divine love, creation, and Christ that felt both deeply rooted and radically open.

“If I had heard Christianity framed this way earlier, I’m not sure I would have felt I needed to leave it to find depth.”

THE MISSING PIECE

I came home with practices. I didn’t have people to practice with.

Retreats can open something in you. Then you come home. Work starts. The phone rings. Kids need something. Traffic happens. And practices that felt so clear on retreat can quietly disappear.

What I kept wishing for was simple: a few people who wanted to keep practicing together, carry it into the week, come back and talk honestly, and encourage one another without pressure or performance.

That is why I’m starting Homeward.

BRING THE DEPTH HOME

Retreat can deepen a practice. Community helps you live it.

Homeward is meant to make contemplative depth accessible in ordinary life—and to give people a community to return to.

Practice together

Learn accessible practices with others.

Live it

Carry them into work, family, relationships, joy, and difficulty.

Return

Talk honestly about what happened. Gentle accountability helps practice become rhythm.

Go deeper

When you’re ready, we can help point toward retreats and deeper formation.

WHY I FEEL CALLED TO DO THIS

What I received, I want to pass on.

So much of what has shaped my life was given to me by teachers, mentors, retreat leaders, writers, spiritual communities, and people who simply made space for me to learn. Most of it was offered generously.

Over time I began to feel that if I simply kept all of that for myself, I would be missing something important. The best way I know to honor the people who have taught me is to give away what I can of what they gave me.

Homeward is one expression of that gratitude. I’m trying to pass along what was freely given to me and open a doorway into practices that are too often hidden away in monasteries, retreats, or specialized communities.

My hope is that people receive something here, make it their own, and carry it farther into the world than I ever could.

A LITTLE ABOUT ME

Study. Practice. Ordinary life.

Study

Religious Studies and Anthropology at UC Santa Barbara, followed by continued theological and spiritual study.

Practice

Decades of meditation, contemplation, retreats, monasteries, teachers, and communities.

Ordinary life

I’m a husband, father, and business leader. Homeward is built for real life because that is where I need these practices too.

I’m not presenting myself as a guru or someone who has spiritual life figured out. I’m a fellow practitioner trying to build the kind of community I kept wishing existed.

Homeward is built to be shared, carried, taught, and eventually given away by many people as the community grows.

IF SOME PART OF THIS STORY FEELS FAMILIAR

Start where you are.

Maybe your faith has changed. Maybe you still feel at home in church. Maybe you’re not sure what you believe anymore. Or maybe you simply want to experience God more deeply. You don’t need to have the answers. You only need enough curiosity to begin.

Learn a practice. Meet a few people. Pay attention to what happens.
