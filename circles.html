Homeward Circles

More than a small group. A guided community of practice.

Homeward Circles bring together the same six to eight people for eight weeks. We practice time-tested forms of Christian meditation and contemplation, explore Scripture and the way of Jesus through lived experience, and support one another in carrying the practice into everyday life. This is the how-to of spiritual life—not another requirement to reach all the same conclusions.

In person in the Fort Worth area or online · Six to eight people · Eight weeks · No cost

The heart of a Circle

Practice changes us. Community helps the practice stay alive.

Many traditional groups offer meaningful teaching, friendship, and support. Homeward Circles build on that foundation by making shared spiritual practice central—not simply another topic to discuss.

A trained facilitator guides the process rather than lecturing or supplying the approved answer. We speak from our own experience, listen without trying to fix one another, explore Scripture with curiosity, and learn from the wisdom that emerges through the group. Between gatherings, an optional private group channel helps with logistics, questions, reminders, and moments of gratitude or wonder.

What makes a Circle different

This isn’t your ordinary small group.

The comparison is not a judgment on what came before. Traditional groups can offer real friendship and learning. Circles build on that foundation by adding the missing how-to: guided practice, shared wisdom, and support for carrying the practice into everyday life.

01

Practice together.

Experience Christian meditation, contemplative prayer, silence, inspired reading, and other time-tested practices together. Sustained practice can reshape patterns of attention and response, helping us become less reactive, more present, and more receptive to God.

02

Learn through shared experience.

The facilitator holds the process, but no one person holds all the answers. Participants speak from lived experience, listen deeply, and discover wisdom together without debating toward agreement.

03

Carry it into life.

Each week includes a simple practice for ordinary life. The Circle helps us keep returning—until we begin to notice God, connection, meaning, awe, and wonder in the moments already in front of us.

The shape of a gathering

A simple rhythm with room to breathe.

The flow shifts from week to week, but every gathering includes simple hospitality, a practice we experience together, Scripture and reflection, honest conversation, and a rhythm to carry into everyday life.

01

Arrive

Settle in over coffee, drinks, and a simple snack. Reconnect and feel at home—no rush.

02

Become present

Practice breath prayer, Christian meditation, silence, gratitude, or another guided contemplative rhythm together.

03

Engage Scripture, a story, or a teaching rooted in the life and way of Jesus with curiosity.

04

Share

Speak from lived experience, listen deeply, and learn from the wisdom in the room. No pressure to agree or perform.

05

Carry it home

Choose a simple practice that helps you return to the present and notice God in everyday life during the week.

A practice you might experience

Here I am. You are here.

In a Circle, we do not only explain breath prayer. We practice it together, reflect on what we notice, and use it during the week whenever we need to return to the present moment.

The point is not to perform the practice perfectly. It is to train attention and become more available to God, ourselves, other people, and the life directly in front of us.

Season One · Finding Home

What one session might feel like.

This shows the tone and flow — not a rigid script. A gathering runs about 90 minutes. Week one is about getting to know each other and feeling welcome: everyone's invited to share, and anyone can pass. There's no pressure to perform.

Week One

The Invitation Home

An approachable first gathering about belonging, spiritual longing, and what it means to begin honestly.

Welcome · ~20 min

Settling in and getting to know each other

We settle in over coffee, tea, and snacks — no rush — then begin with a simple question: What drew you here? Everyone's welcome to share, or to pass.

Practice · ~8 min

A guided breath prayer

A simple practice that calms the body, steadies the mind, and helps us become present to the presence of God — with us and within us. As we breathe, we grow more grounded, open, and loving.
Breathing in: Here I am.
Breathing out: You are here.

Explore · ~15 min

A story and reflection

A short, familiar story Jesus told — the son who comes home to a father running to meet him — and an open, honest conversation about God, grace, and how each of us hears it.

Conversation · ~30 min

Honest conversation

  • When you hear the word home, what image or longing comes to mind?
  • Where do you most long to experience welcome or belonging?
  • What do you make of a love that runs to meet us before we've figured it all out?
For the week · ~5 min

A practice to carry home

A few minutes of breath prayer on a few days this week, using the same words: Here I am. You are here.

Blessing · ~5 min

A simple blessing

We each offer a word for how we're leaving, and close with a blessing: May you know you are received before you have everything figured out — and may you notice the presence of God on the road ahead.

Is this for me?

The right fit is a posture of openness—not certainty.

You may feel at home if...

  • You are seeking depth, connection, and a more lived spiritual life.
  • You are open to God, a Higher Power, or the possibility of something beyond yourself.
  • You are willing to explore Jesus and Scripture respectfully, even if your beliefs are unsettled.
  • You can speak from your own experience and listen without needing to correct others.
  • You are willing to practice between gatherings and support others on the journey.

A Circle is probably not the right fit if...

  • Your primary purpose is to debate, disprove, convert, or require agreement from others.
  • You are unwilling to participate respectfully in Jesus-centered practices.
  • You want a class built around one expert supplying the correct answers.
  • You are looking for group therapy or professional mental-health treatment.
  • You cannot respect confidentiality or the lived experience of other participants.
  • You are looking for a drop-in group rather than an eight-week shared journey.
