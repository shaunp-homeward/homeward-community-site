The Journey of Faith

A Spiral, Not a Ladder

Spiritual growth is rarely linear. We receive, question, seek, rebuild, embody, and awaken - often more than once. No stage is a rank, and no stage makes anyone more loved by God.

The stages describe how our relationship to faith may change - not God's relationship to us. Jesus remains at the center of the journey.

Where You Are

You are not failing. You are becoming honest.

The questions may be intellectual, emotional, moral, or deeply personal. Sometimes the hardest part is not the question itself, but the fear of what asking it might cost.

One common risk is believing you must solve everything quickly - or carry the questions alone. The invitation is to tell the truth in the presence of God and trustworthy people, without forcing a conclusion before it is ready.

1

Your questions may be a form of faithfulness.

Love of truth can require the courage to question answers that no longer hold.

2

Grief often travels with doubt.

You may be mourning certainty, community, identity, or a picture of God that once made life feel safer.

3

You do not owe anyone a rushed answer.

Some questions need study. Others need healing, time, or a new experience of God.

4

Honesty needs companionship and practice.

Safe people and simple prayer keep questions connected to relationship rather than isolation.

A Practice for This Season

Honest Prayer

Bringing your whole truth into God's presence.

1

Arrive

Take one slow breath. Pray: “God, here is what is true.”

2

Name

Say or write the question, feeling, or disappointment without softening it for God.

3

Ask

What do you need? What do you wish God would do? Let the prayer be specific and unfinished.

4

Notice & Rest

Notice any thread of trust or longing that remains. Close with silence or: “I believe; help my unbelief.”

WORDS YOU CAN BORROW

God, I do not understand...and I am still here.
I am angry because...listen to what is true.
I want to trust, but...help my unbelief.

Try this for seven days: write one honest sentence each day. The aim is not certainty. It is relationship.
