# Homeward V9 Audience, Movement & Messaging Strategy Snapshot

Date: 2026-08-22
Status: Working strategy for testing; V8 remains the protected fallback.

## Strategic conclusion

Homeward does not have one primary customer. It has a primary participant and a primary growth engine.

- The participant is the person whose life and experience Homeward must serve.
- The growth engine is a trusted church or community leader who can experience Homeward, trust it, bring others into it, and eventually reproduce it.

Internal name for this leader: **Homeward Multiplier**.

> A trusted church or community leader who already gathers or influences people, personally longs for deeper formation, is open to contemplative practice and honest questions, and could carry a Homeward Circle into an existing community.

## Movement thesis

Homeward should grow as a distributed Jesus-centered community-of-practice movement. Founder-led Circles establish the experience and culture. Every Circle forms participants, and some participants become potential leaders. Those leaders apprentice, learn to protect the essential Homeward container, and then facilitate Circles in churches, homes, recovery communities, and other networks. Churches accelerate distribution without owning Homeward; Homeward strengthens churches without attempting to replace them.

### Formation flywheel

Show up -> Practice -> Notice -> Share -> Belong -> Practice again

### Movement flywheel

Experience -> Believe in it -> Invite others -> Apprentice -> Lead -> Multiply

### Recommended leader pathway

Experience -> Apprentice -> Co-lead -> Lead -> Remain connected through an ongoing Homeward Leader Circle

## Audience

### Primary growth audience

Church and community leaders with trusted access to existing groups who can experience, champion, host, lead, or multiply Homeward Circles.

Priority roles:
1. Spiritual-formation / discipleship leaders
2. Denominational multiplication / innovation leaders
3. Senior and executive pastors
4. Small-group / adult-ministry leaders
5. Recovery-community founders and pastors
6. Trusted lay leaders and spiritual directors
7. Network connectors and coaches who can open multiple doors

### Secondary audience

Adults seeking a deeper spiritual life, authentic connection, and practical ways to experience and live their faith, whether confident, questioning, or somewhere between.

## Core positioning

### Brand promise

**A deeper spiritual life takes practice. And people to practice with.**

### Participant value proposition

Homeward Circles help people build a deeper spiritual life through Jesus-centered contemplative practices, Scripture, and honest community they can carry into everyday life.

### Leader value proposition

Experience a Circle yourself, be nourished by the practices, and discover a repeatable way to create deeper formation and connection for others.

### Church value proposition

Homeward gives churches a free, low-lift way to add contemplative practice, deeper listening, and experiential formation to what they already offer.

### Differentiator

Homeward makes shared practice a central part of spiritual community, not just something people hear about and try to remember later.

### Complementary framing

Many groups help us learn. Homeward adds another dimension: a place to practice together.

Avoid framing Homeward as a correction to church, traditional small groups, Bible study, or teaching.

## Hero direction for V9 test

### Headline

A deeper spiritual life takes practice.

### Emphasis line

And people to practice with.

### Hero body

Homeward Circles are small, guided, Jesus-centered communities where contemplative prayer, meditation, Scripture, silence, reflection, and honest conversation become practices we learn together. Over time, these spiritual exercises help us learn to become more present to God, ourselves, and one another - more peaceful, less reactive, more connected, and increasingly able to carry the love and way of Jesus into ordinary life. Begin with one free four-week season.

Important: preserve the longer transformational benefit language. Do not reduce the hero to a short feature description.

## Practices positioning

Use both concepts:

### Primary concept

**Spiritual exercises for the inner life.**

### Supporting line

**Ancient practices for everyday life.**

Reasoning: “spiritual exercises” communicates repetition, training, formation, and embodied practice. It also has a strong Ignatian association, so Homeward should not imply that its entire program is the Ignatian Spiritual Exercises. “Ancient practices” keeps the broader Christian lineage clear.

Recommended explanation:

Just as physical exercise strengthens the body through repetition, spiritual exercises train attention, openness, presence, and love. Homeward draws from ancient Christian practices - contemplative prayer, meditation, Scripture, gratitude, silence, and reflection - and makes them simple enough to practice in ordinary life. The goal is not mastery of a technique, but becoming more present to God and others, more peaceful and less reactive, more connected, joyful, loving, and ready to serve.

## Church page core messages

1. Homeward complements church rather than competes with it.
2. The initial pilot is free.
3. Homeward facilitates the first Circle.
4. Leaders may participate rather than perform.
5. The church does not need to buy or build a curriculum.
6. At four weeks there is a natural pause.
7. If useful, a participant can begin a leader pathway.
8. Jesus and Scripture remain central while honest questions are welcome.
9. Homeward maintains its identity through co-branding.

## Co-branding recommendation

Default naming:

**Homeward Circle at [Church Name]**

or

**Hosted by [Church Name] · A Homeward Circle**

Avoid white-labeling during the pilot stage.

Guiding posture:

> Take the practices freely. If you are calling it a Homeward Circle, steward the Homeward way.

## What must remain standardized

- Homeward identity and naming
- Jesus-centered and spacious posture
- Leader as participant, not answer-giver
- Contemplative practice in every gathering
- Scripture integrated as encounter as well as study
- Permission to pass
- No fixing, debating, or unsolicited advice
- Deep listening
- Practice carried into ordinary life
- Group-size guardrails
- Feedback and safeguarding expectations

## What may adapt

- Location and schedule
- Hospitality
- Contextual examples
- Specific practices from an approved library
- Church-specific introduction
- Co-branding
- Some Scripture selections
- Local application

## Homepage architecture recommendation

Keep the homepage participant-first because church leaders will first ask whether their people would want the experience.

Recommended order:
1. Hero: human longing + practice
2. Recognition
3. What makes a Circle different
4. Finding Home: four-week front door
5. Simple joining process
6. For Churches & Communities
7. Spiritual Exercises / Practices
8. Founder trust
9. Fit + concise FAQ
10. Interest form

Move deeper research, journey reflection, and institutional details to dedicated pages so the homepage does not carry too many messages.

## V1 learning agenda

The first pilots should test:
1. Is a Circle meaningfully different from other small groups?
2. Is four weeks enough to experience value?
3. Which practices continue between gatherings?
4. Does Jesus-centered plus room for honest questions create trust?
5. Are pastors and leaders personally nourished enough to become champions?
6. Do church leaders see Homeward as complementary rather than competitive?
7. Does a healthy Circle naturally reveal plausible future facilitators?
8. Does co-branding preserve Homeward identity while creating church trust?

## V1 scorecard

Participant measures:
- Desire to return
- Likelihood to recommend
- Days per week a Homeward practice was used
- Sense of belonging
- Quality of listening and conversation
- Perceived spiritual depth / attentiveness to God
- What mattered most?
- What should change?

Leader measures:
- Would host another Circle: Yes / Maybe / No
- Would consider leading or co-leading: Yes / Maybe / No
- Has another leader to introduce: Yes / Maybe / No

Behavioral measures:
- Returned
- Practiced
- Referred
- Hosted
- Apprenticed
- Led

## Implementation principle

Do not redesign Homeward around institutional buyers. Preserve the participant desire and experience, add a clear church/community pathway, test with real leaders, and use actual pilot behavior to drive the next messaging revision.
