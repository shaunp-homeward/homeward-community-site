GET A TASTE

You can describe an orange.
Or you can taste it.

You can talk about its color, texture, and sweetness. Tasting the fruit brings a kind of understanding that description alone cannot give. Prayer has an experiential dimension, too. Homeward’s goal is to help your group get a taste of contemplative prayer, with guidance that makes unfamiliar practices approachable.

01

Discover a practice

Hear a simple introduction: where the practice comes from, what it invites, and how to begin. No contemplative experience is required.

02

Experience it together

Your group can experiment with new forms of prayer in short, guided introductions. You can participate at your own pace or sit out a practice.

03

Reflect and carry it home

Notice what happened, ask questions, and choose something to try in daily life. There is no particular feeling you have to produce.

APPROACHABLE WAYS TO PRAY

Ancient Christian practices.
A gentle place to begin.

We choose practices with your group’s background and hopes in mind. These are some of the forms of prayer we may introduce.

Centering Prayer

A form of silent Christian prayer that invites us to consent to God’s presence and action. A sacred word serves as a gentle reminder of that intention when we notice ourselves caught up in thoughts.

Father Thomas Keating helped develop and share this approach. Contemplative Outreach supports its teaching and practice.

Learn about Centering Prayer

The Examen

A prayerful way to look back over the day with God. Notice gifts, give thanks, bring difficult moments honestly into prayer, and consider how to respond with love tomorrow.

We guide a simple reflection so people can experience it together and try it at home.

Scripture & guided imagination

Use imagination to enter a Gospel scene: picture the setting, notice Jesus, and listen prayerfully to the passage. Guided visualizations stay grounded in Scripture and the life of Jesus.

People can also choose to listen quietly or reflect on the text without forming an image.

We also introduce simple breath prayer and ways of listening with care. The aim is a deeper relationship with God and a growing capacity to love.

QUESTIONS FROM CHURCH LEADERS

A clear beginning. Room for questions.

Is Homeward Christian?

Yes. Jesus is at the center. The experiences draw from Scripture and Christian contemplative practice, with prayer and love of God and neighbor shaping our purpose.

What if contemplative prayer is new to our group?

We explain each practice and introduce it gently. No prior experience is needed. Questions are welcome, and people may sit out a practice or choose how much to share.

Will we need to adopt a new program or train leaders?

No. Homeward facilitates a short experience within your existing community. There is no requirement to start another group, pursue leader training, or continue afterward.

Is there a charge or a commitment to finish?

No charge and no obligation to complete every session. Four gatherings, or three for Sacred Listening, is the intended rhythm. Your group can stop at any time.

Who is this for?

Existing Christian small groups, care teams, staff teams, and ministry leaders who want to explore prayer and listening together. A first conversation helps us discern whether the experience fits your group.
