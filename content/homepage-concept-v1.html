The Invitation

Does any of this feel familiar?

Maybe you know a lot about spiritual life—or maybe you simply want something deeper. Either way, understanding faith and actually living it are not quite the same thing.

You know things about God, but you long to experience God more deeply—not simply gather more information.

You pray, and sometimes it still feels like talking to the ceiling. You wonder if prayer can also become quieter and more receptive.

You may have read Richard Rohr, Henri Nouwen, Thomas Merton, or John Mark Comer—and still wish you had people to actually practice with.

You carry real questions and want to explore them honestly without being pushed toward a predetermined conclusion.

You want to be less reactive and more awake—more peaceful, joyful, loving, and rooted in God rather than self-improvement.

Any one of these is enough to begin.

Jesus at the center

Traditional groups can offer meaningful friendship, teaching, and Scripture study. Homeward adds another layer: guided practice, lived experience, and a rhythm that continues between gatherings.

PRACTICES FOR THE MIND AND HEART

Ancient practices. Everyday change.

We exercise our bodies because strength does not appear simply because we understand it. Spiritual practices work in a similar way: repeated prayer, meditation, gratitude, Scripture, and reflection train attention, openness, presence, and love.

The point is not to become good at meditation. The point is to become more present, peaceful, joyful, resilient, loving—and rooted in God.

WHY HOMEWARD EXISTS

I came home with practices. I didn’t have people to practice with.

COULD HOMEWARD BE A FIT?

Openness matters more than certainty.

You do not have to arrive with settled beliefs. You do need a willingness to participate respectfully, practice, and listen.

You may feel at home if…

  • You are seeking depth, connection, and a more lived spiritual life.
  • You are willing to explore Jesus and Scripture respectfully, even if your beliefs are unsettled.
  • You can speak from your own experience, listen without fixing, and practice between gatherings.

A Circle may not be the best fit if…

  • Your primary purpose is to debate, disprove, convert, or require agreement.
  • You want a class built around one expert supplying the correct answers.
  • You are not willing to respect confidentiality or the lived experience of other participants.

Common Questions

Questions are welcome. Here are a few clear answers.

What do you mean by “rooted in Jesus”?

We return continually to the life, teachings, and way of Jesus as the center of our shared practice. People do not have to arrive with identical beliefs or settled certainty, but they should be sincerely open to exploring Jesus and participating respectfully in a Jesus-centered community.

What if the word “God” is difficult for me?

You are welcome to begin honestly. Some people come with a clear Christian faith. Others are questioning, rebuilding, or more comfortable speaking about a Higher Power or an openness to something beyond themselves. Homeward remains rooted in Jesus, but no one is required to pretend certainty or use identical language.

What are Christian meditation and contemplative prayer?

They are time-tested ways of becoming quiet, attentive, and open to God. Practices may use the breath, a prayer word, Scripture, reflection, visualization, gratitude, or silent presence. No prior experience is needed; we learn by practicing together.

Do you draw from traditions outside Christianity?

Homeward is rooted in Jesus and draws primarily from the historic Christian contemplative tradition. We also acknowledge that practices such as silence, attention, compassion, gratitude, and awareness of the breath appear in other traditions. We engage those points of connection honestly and respectfully without turning Homeward into a blend of religions.

Is Homeward a church?

Homeward is forming as a Jesus-centered spiritual community, beginning with Circles. You do not need to leave an existing church, and you do not need previous church involvement to participate.

Why is a conversation required before joining a Circle?

The conversation gives you a chance to ask questions and understand the experience before committing. It also helps us learn what you are seeking, confirm that the Circle posture is a good fit, and thoughtfully match people into groups. We are looking for openness and a desire to grow—not doctrinal certainty.
