OUR STORY · SHAUN PENNINGTON

A faith I could
actually live.

Homeward began with a question that kept returning: what helps us practice the life Jesus describes?

I grew up in the Christian faith. I still value what formed me there: Jesus, Scripture, prayer, service, and the conviction that faith should matter in the way we live.

But knowing more did not always mean becoming more loving. I wanted a faith that could meet me in conflict, pressure, uncertainty, and ordinary days. I wanted to experience God more deeply and learn to respond with greater presence and compassion.

That longing became a long search—and, eventually, the beginning of Homeward.

THIRTY YEARS OF SEARCHING

Learning to be still.
Learning to pay attention.

My formation has included Religious Studies and Anthropology, decades of prayer and meditation, extended retreats, monasteries, silence, and learning in community.

I also spent meaningful time learning from Buddhist contemplative traditions. I value that experience while recognizing that traditions differ in what they teach. The search broadened my understanding and helped me see how deeply our habits of attention shape the way we live.

Retreat could open something in me. Daily practice helped me return to it. Over time, I became less interested in another powerful experience and more interested in what helps us become less reactive, more honest, and more capable of love.

Teaching and theology matter. I came to believe that formation also needs practice: repeated ways of praying, listening, reflecting, and showing up with God and one another. I wanted those practices to have a home in everyday community, where people could learn together.

RETURNING TO JESUS

The deeper I went,
the more I returned.

Jesus remained the deepest center of my spiritual life.

As I explored presence, surrender, forgiveness, and compassion, I found myself returning to the Gospels and to the way Jesus lived with people. His invitation to love God and love our neighbors became a touchstone for what I was learning.

The Christian contemplative tradition gave me language and practices for that depth. Silence, receptive prayer, meditation, and slow attention to Scripture could help faith become a lived relationship with God.

For me, returning to Jesus meant bringing the search into ordinary relationships. A practice mattered if it helped me listen more patiently, forgive more freely, and notice another person with care. I began to understand contemplation as a way of making room for God’s love to shape my attention and response. That is the connection I want Homeward to help people explore together.

Thomas Keating’s teaching on Centering Prayer, Thomas Merton’s writing, and retreats with Father Laurence Freeman helped me find that path. Richard Rohr and Dallas Willard also helped connect inner formation with a life of love.

Those voices describe my formation; they do not define a set of beliefs everyone must share. Homeward is intentionally Jesus-centered, rooted in Christian contemplative practice, and open to honest questions. We keep asking what helps us become more available to God and more loving toward others.

SERVING THE COMMUNITY ALREADY THERE

Good groups.
A deeper shared practice.

Churches already have small groups, care teams, recovery communities, and people who know and trust one another. I began to wonder what might happen if those relationships had more room for contemplative prayer and deeper listening.

Homeward now begins by coming alongside existing Christian groups. A four-week group experience introduces prayer, Sacred Listening, reflection, and everyday practice. A three-session Sacred Listening workshop offers a focused way to become more present to one another.

We facilitate a short season, then leave the group with practices it can continue. Your community keeps its leadership, relationships, and identity. The hope is to strengthen what is already there and help faith become more fully lived.

FORMATION IN REAL LIFE

The practices have
to work at home too.

Lorraine and I have been together for twenty years, married for sixteen, and are raising two teenagers. Marriage, parenting, friendship, work, and responsibility have been some of my deepest places of formation.

It is one thing to feel peaceful on retreat. It is another to stay present when someone needs you, a conversation gets difficult, or life does not go as planned. Homeward belongs in that world.

I bring more than thirty years of contemplative study and practice, a BA in Religious Studies and Anthropology from UC Santa Barbara, and twenty-five years of business leadership. Coaching training and experience facilitating YPO and Vistage peer groups have shaped how I make room for honest conversation.

What I hope to offer is thoughtful facilitation, humility, and care: enough structure for people to feel supported, enough space to listen, and a willingness to keep learning together.
